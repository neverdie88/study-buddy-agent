# Fractions of a Shape (Term 4, Weeks 1-2)

## What is a fraction?
A fraction is a part of a whole that has been cut into EQUAL parts.
The bottom number (denominator) tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into.
The top number (numerator) tells how many parts we are talking about.
Mrs. Lee calls it "pizza maths": if a pizza is cut into 4 equal slices and you eat 1,
you ate 1/4 (one quarter).

## Halves, quarters and thirds
- 1/2 (one half): the whole is cut into 2 equal parts.
- 1/4 (one quarter): the whole is cut into 4 equal parts.
- 1/3 (one third): the whole is cut into 3 equal parts.
- 2/4 is the same amount as 1/2. We check by drawing two pizzas the same size.

## Fractions on a number line
Draw a line from 0 to 1. Cut it into equal jumps. For quarters, make 4 equal jumps:
0, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1.

# Adding 2-Digit Numbers with Regrouping

## Mrs. Lee's "Ones First" method
1. Line up the tens and the ones in columns.
2. Add the ONES first. If you get 10 or more, make a ten! Write the ones and
   carry the 1 ten to the tens column.
3. Add the TENS, including the ten you carried.

Example: 27 + 15. Ones: 7 + 5 = 12, write 2 and carry 1 ten.
Tens: 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. Answer: 42.
We use base-ten blocks (sticks of ten and little cubes) to show regrouping.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting to carry the ten.
- Cutting a shape into parts that are NOT equal and calling them quarters.
